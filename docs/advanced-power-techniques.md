# Advanced Power Techniques: Tricks 89-100+

Where experts operate—beyond the obvious.

## 15+ Advanced Techniques

### 89. Role-Play & Devil's Advocate Mode
**Get opposing perspectives** by assigning specific personas.

**Role-play structure:**
- Assign character: "You are [specific expert with different perspective]"
- Give constraints: [What this person believes/values]
- Define context: [Specific situation they're evaluating]
- Request response: [How they'd think about this]

**Devil's advocate specifically:**
- Task: "Argue the opposite of what I believe"
- Constraint: "Argue it as persuasively as possible"
- Goal: "Find the strongest version of the other side"

**Example:**
```
I believe [my position].

Play devil's advocate—argue the opposite position 
as persuasively as you can. I want to understand 
the strongest version of the other side, not a strawman.
```

### 90. Future Prediction Prompts
**Explore what could happen** by reasoning through scenarios.

**Future prediction structure:**
1. Current state: [What's true now]
2. Key variables: [What could change]
3. Timeline: [Year/timeframe]
4. Assumptions: [What you're assuming]
5. Scenario 1: [If X happens]
   - What changes
   - Implications
   - How likely (% probability)
6. Scenario 2: [If Y happens]
7. Scenario 3: [If Z happens]
8. Most likely: [What probably happens]

**Example:**
```
Predict what [industry/technology/society] looks like in [years].

Current state: [What exists now]
Key trends: [What's changing]

Give me:
1. Three different futures (optimistic, realistic, pessimistic)
2. What would need to be true for each
3. Which is most likely and why
4. Signals to watch that indicate which direction we're heading
5. What should [person/company] do to prepare
```

### 91. Ask "What Am I Missing?"
**Identify blind spots** in your thinking or planning.

**Blind spot discovery:**
- Summary: [Your plan/thinking]
- Ask: "What am I missing? What assumptions am I making?"
- Request: "Give me 5 things I haven't considered"

**Better follow-up:**
- "What would an expert in [field] say I'm overlooking?"
- "What conventional wisdom am I not questioning?"
- "What worst-case scenarios haven't I considered?"

**Example:**
```
I'm planning to [decision/project].

Here's my thinking:
- Goal: [What I'm trying to achieve]
- Approach: [How I'm doing it]
- Expected outcome: [What should happen]
- Timeline: [When]

What am I missing?
- What assumptions am I making?
- What could go wrong?
- What aren't I seeing?
- What would experts say I overlooked?
```

### 92. Constraint-Based Creativity
**Generate ideas by adding specific limits** (counterintuitively, constraints spark creativity).

**Constraint examples:**
- Budget limit: "Under $1,000"
- Time limit: "Must take less than 1 hour"
- Resource limit: "Using only these tools"
- Audience limit: "For people who don't know anything about this"
- Format limit: "Using only emoji"
- Length limit: "In exactly 3 words"

**Example:**
```
Generate [quantity] ideas for [challenge].

Constraints:
- Budget: [Specific amount or "free"]
- Time: [How long to implement]
- Resources: [What you have access to]
- Format: [How it should look]
- Audience: [Who needs to understand it]
- Outcome: [What should happen]

Give me creative ideas that work within these limits.
Rank by feasibility and impact.
```

### 93. Reverse-Engineer Success
**Deconstruct how successful things work** to copy the pattern.

**Reverse engineering process:**
1. Successful example: [What worked]
2. Dissect: What made it work?
   - Who was it for (audience)
   - What was the core offer
   - How was it communicated
   - What made it compelling
   - What was the mechanism
3. Pattern: What's the underlying principle?
4. Apply: How could you use this pattern?

**Example:**
```
Reverse-engineer [successful thing] to understand how it works.

What worked: [Example: viral post, successful campaign, great product]
Audience: [Who loved it]
The appeal: [Why people engaged/bought/shared]

Deconstruct:
- What's the core principle here?
- What made it different from alternatives?
- How did they communicate it?
- What psychological buttons did it press?
- What mechanics made it work?

Pattern: [What's the underlying formula]

How could you apply this pattern to [your situation]?
```

### 94. Generate Expert Personas
**Create detailed profiles** of expert perspectives.

**Expert persona structure:**
- Name: [Specific expert, real or fictional]
- Background: [Their experience, education]
- Beliefs: [Core principles they hold]
- Perspective: [How they'd view situations]
- Communication style: [How they talk]
- Likely advice: [What they'd recommend]

**Example:**
```
Generate expert personas for understanding [topic].

Create 3 different expert perspectives:
1. Expert A: [Type of expert, background]
   - Their core beliefs
   - How they'd approach [problem]
   - Their likely recommendations
   - Their potential blind spots

2. Expert B: [Different type of expert]
3. Expert C: [Third perspective]

For each: How would they view [situation differently]?
Where would they agree and disagree?
```

### 95. Stress-Test Your Idea
**Probe for weaknesses** before you invest in something.

**Stress testing structure:**
1. The idea: [What you're considering]
2. Test 1: Reduce price by 50%. Does it still work?
3. Test 2: Double the timeline. Any problems?
4. Test 3: Cut the team in half. Still feasible?
5. Test 4: What if a competitor copies this?
6. Test 5: What if market conditions change?
7. Test 6: What if key assumption is wrong?
8. Verdict: What's truly fragile? What's solid?

**Example:**
```
Stress-test this idea: [Your plan/product/business]

Core proposition: [What you're trying to do]
Key assumptions: [What needs to be true]

Stress test by exploring:
1. What if costs are 2x higher than expected?
2. What if it takes 3x longer to build?
3. What if customers are less interested than you think?
4. What if a well-funded competitor enters the market?
5. What if one key person leaves the team?
6. What if market conditions shift?

For each: How fragile is your idea? What breaks?

Verdict: What's the idea's real weakness?
```

### 96. Build Custom Frameworks
**Create reusable decision frameworks** tailored to your needs.

**Framework structure:**
1. Purpose: [What decision does this help with]
2. Inputs: [What information do you need]
3. Criteria: [How do you evaluate]
4. Process: [Step-by-step thinking]
5. Outputs: [What decision comes out]

**Example:**
```
Build a decision framework for [recurring decisions you make].

Situation: [When would you use this]
Decision needed: [What are you deciding]
What matters: [What criteria matter most]
Common mistakes: [What people get wrong]

Create a framework that:
- Takes specific inputs (what you need to know)
- Walks through systematic evaluation
- Leads to clear decision
- Includes safety checks (don't miss this)

Make it repeatable so you use it each time.
```

### 97. What Would [Expert] Say?
**Consult imaginary experts** for perspective and validation.

**Expert consultation:**
- Question: [What you're deciding]
- Expert 1: "What would [expert name] say?"
- Expert 2: "What would [different expert] say?"
- Expert 3: "What would [contrarian expert] say?"

**Example:**
```
I'm considering [decision].

What would these people say:
1. [Expert in this field]: How would they think about this?
2. [Business person]: What's their practical advice?
3. [Skeptic/critic]: What would they warn me about?
4. [Visionary]: What's the bigger opportunity?

Give me each perspective authentically.
Then: What's the synthesis? What should I actually do?
```

### 98. Prompt Chaining Mastery
**Sequence prompts strategically** to build sophisticated outcomes.

**Prompt chain structure:**
```
Prompt 1: Gather information
→ Prompt 2: Analyze what you found
→ Prompt 3: Generate options based on analysis
→ Prompt 4: Evaluate tradeoffs
→ Prompt 5: Create implementation plan
```

**Why chaining works:**
- Each prompt builds on previous output
- You can adjust mid-chain based on results
- Complexity emerges from simple sequences
- Better than trying to do everything at once

**Example:**
```
Help me chain prompts to accomplish [complex goal].

Goal: [What you're trying to achieve]
Starting point: [What you know/have]
Ideal output: [What you need at the end]

Create a prompt chain:
1. Prompt 1: [What should I ask first?]
2. Prompt 2: [What builds on that?]
3. Prompt 3: [What comes next?]
4. Prompt 4: [Then?]
5. Prompt 5: [Final step?]

For each, tell me:
- What I'm asking
- Why this order
- What I'll get
- How to use that output in the next prompt
```

### 99. Iterate Relentlessly
**Treat first draft as starting point**, not finished work.

**Iteration cycle:**
1. First pass: Get something down (don't aim for perfect)
2. Evaluate: What's good? What's not?
3. Revise: Make specific improvements
4. Evaluate again: Better? What else?
5. Refine: Polish the good parts
6. Repeat until satisfied

**Example:**
```
I want to improve [output/writing/idea].

Current version: [What you have]
What's working: [What's good]
What's not: [What needs improvement]

Help me iterate:
1. First feedback: What's the top thing to improve?
2. Revised version: [I'll make changes]
3. Next feedback: What else?
4. Repeat 2-3 more times until it's really good

Guide me through several rounds of refinement.
```

**Workflow example:**

```
Workflow:

5. [Expert consultation] What would [experts] say?
6. [Framework] Apply my decision framework
7. [Iterate] Refine based on all input
8. [Action] Create implementation plan
```

**Example:**
```
Create a workflow combining multiple prompt techniques
for [specific challenge you face repeatedly].

Challenge: [What you're trying to solve]
Desired outcome: [What success looks like]
Constraints: [What you're working with]

Design a workflow that:
1. Uses [Technique 1] to start
2. Builds to [Technique 2]
3. Incorporates [Technique 3]
4. Applies [Technique 4]
5. Results in [desired outcome]

For each step:
- What am I doing
- What do I get

```

## Real-World Example

**Generic request:**
```
Help me think about this problem.
```

**Advanced power approach:**
```
I'm facing [decision/challenge].

Here's my current thinking:
- Situation: [What's happening]
- My assumption: [What I believe is true]
- My initial direction: [Where I'm headed]

I want to think about this deeply, so:

1. Reverse-engineer success: Show me how others solved this
2. Stress-test my assumption: What if I'm wrong?
3. Devil's advocate: Argue for the opposite direction strongly
4. Expert consultation: What would [expert 1], [expert 2], [expert 3] say?
5. What am I missing?: What blind spots do I have?
6. Synthesize: Given all this, what's the best path?
7. Iterate: Let me refine based on your feedback

Walk me through this thinking systematically.
```

**The difference:** Using layers of advanced techniques creates thinking depth that catches things surface-level analysis misses.

## Principles for Advanced Use

1. **Combine techniques, don't just apply individually**
   - Single techniques give good results
   - Layered techniques reveal hidden patterns

2. **Iterate on your prompts as much as the output**
   - First prompt is rarely the best prompt
   - Refine based on what you learn

3. **Use constraints to spark creativity**
   - Limits focus thinking
   - Focus produces better ideas

4. **Treat disagreement as information**
   - Devil's advocate reveals real weaknesses
   - Opposing views sharpen your thinking

5. **Build custom tools for recurring problems**
   - Frameworks pay dividends over time
   - Workflows compound in value

6. **Think in systems, not steps**
   - One-off prompts help with tasks
   - Workflows help with transformation

7. **Always ask "What am I missing?"**
   - Last 10% of thinking catches blind spots
   - Blind spots cause biggest failures
